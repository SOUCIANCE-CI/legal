DPIA support pack

Missing Time for Jira. Version 1, 18 October 2026.

Promised by section 9.2 of the Data Processing Addendum, which commits us to assist you under Article 28(3)(f) GDPR.

This is not your DPIA. The assessment is yours to carry out and to sign, because you decide why the app is used, on whom, and what happens to its output. What follows is the half of the work we can do for you: an accurate description of the processing, and an honest list of the risks we can see from where we sit.

1. Will you need a DPIA at all?

Probably yes. Against the WP248 rev.01 criteria, this processing meets at least four:

  • Evaluation or scoring. The app counts consecutive weeks in which a person logged less than expected and highlights anyone short three weeks running.
  • Systematic monitoring. A scheduled scan runs daily and keeps 26 weeks of records.
  • Vulnerable data subjects. WP248 names employees specifically, because of the imbalance of power in the employment relationship.
  • Matching or combining datasets. Jira worklogs are compared against an expectation and a holiday calendar that you configure.

Two criteria normally suffice to presume a DPIA is required. Several national supervisory authorities also list employee-monitoring processing on their Article 35(4) lists. We would plan on doing one.

2. Description of the processing

2.1 Data flow

  1. A Jira administrator opens the report. The app reads the site's active user accounts and saves a roster of account IDs and display names.
  2. The app queries Jira for worklogs in the selected date range. Each worklog object is reduced to three fields on arrival: author account ID, start date, duration. Everything else, including any free-text comment the employee wrote and the author's profile fields, is discarded at that point.
  3. Hours are totalled per person per day and compared against the expectation you configured, after deducting weekends, your configured public holidays and any per-person exemption.
  4. Once daily, a scheduled job repeats the comparison for the last completed week and records who fell short, by how much. It keeps 26 weeks.
  5. From that history the app derives, per person, the number of consecutive recent weeks they were short.

2.2 What is stored, and for how long

Three keys in Atlassian Forge storage, inside the infrastructure serving your own site. Full detail in the Privacy Policy and Annex I.B. In summary: your settings; a roster of account IDs and names; and 26 weeks of per-week shortfall records. No worklog is ever copied.

2.3 Who can see it

Jira administrators only. The report, the settings and the manual scan are all restricted, enforced on the server rather than hidden in the interface.

2.4 Necessity and proportionality

Points you will probably want in your own assessment:

  • The app reads data your organisation already holds. It creates no new collection of employee data, and adds no tracking, no screenshots, no keystrokes, no location and no activity monitoring.
  • It stores no worklog. What it keeps is a comparison, not a record of what people did.
  • Retention is 26 weeks and is enforced by age, not by volume.
  • Access is limited to administrators, which is narrower than the Jira worklog data itself, visible to anyone who can see the issue.
  • The app takes no decision. It produces a figure for a human to interpret.

3. Risks, and what reduces them

Written from the vendor's side. You will have risks we cannot see, arising from how you use it.

RiskWhy it is realWhat reduces it
A number is wrong and someone is blamed for it The app knows nothing about leave, sickness, part-time arrangements you have not configured, or work done outside Jira. A person on holiday looks identical to a person who did not log. Per-person hours and exclusions, a holiday calendar, and a visible flag on implausible days. Section 6 of the Licence Agreement states the output must not be the sole basis for a pay or disciplinary decision. Configure leave before you deploy, not after the first complaint.
Function creep towards discipline A consecutive-weeks counter is designed to make a pattern visible. The step from "visible" to "evidence in a disciplinary file" is short and nobody has to decide to take it. Decide in advance what the output is for and write it down. If the purpose is chasing missing timesheets, the counter is proportionate; if it becomes performance assessment, that is a different purpose needing its own basis and its own assessment.
Inference of special category data Repeated shortfalls on particular days can suggest a medical condition, a pregnancy, a religious observance, or caring responsibilities. The app does not know this; a manager reading the grid might. No special category data is requested or stored. The exposure is in interpretation, which is why access is limited to administrators and why the policy says plainly that the app only compares two numbers.
Exports leave the perimeter The export produces a named list, ready to paste into a chat or a spreadsheet. Once pasted it is outside Jira, outside Forge and outside any retention rule. Export is restricted to administrators. Beyond that it is yours to govern: decide who may export, where it may be pasted, and how long it is kept.
Transparency failure towards employees People are measured weekly by software they may not know exists. That is a transparency problem before it is a security one. The "How the app evaluates people" section of our Privacy Policy is written to be readable by the people being measured, and may be reused in your own notice.
Former employees persist in the history Someone who left four months ago remains named in records of weeks that already happened. Records age out at 26 weeks. Removing someone from Jira or excluding them in Settings stops them appearing in new reports.
Vendor outside the EEA We are in Côte d'Ivoire, a country without an adequacy decision. Standard Contractual Clauses and a published transfer impact assessment. We hold no copy of your data, so there is nothing at our end for an authority to seize.

4. What you must do, and we cannot

  • Choose a lawful basis. Consent is generally not valid between employer and employee because of the imbalance of power. Measuring working time may rest on a legal obligation in your country; the consecutive-weeks measure is a separate purpose and will usually need legitimate interest with a documented balancing test.
  • Consult employee representatives where required. In several countries deploying a time-monitoring tool engages the works council, and in some of them data gathered without that step cannot lawfully be used at all. This is the step most often skipped and the most expensive to skip.
  • Inform the individuals under Articles 13 and 14, including about the weekly history and the consecutive-weeks measure.
  • Keep a human in the loop. No decision with legal or similarly significant effects, including any disciplinary measure, may rest solely on the app's output.
  • Keep the roster current by opening the app after a departure.
