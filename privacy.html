Write to contact@souciance.com for anything in this policy. For a suspected security problem, use the same address with [SECURITY] in the subject; that channel is checked daily.

Two different roles

This matters more than it sounds, because the answers differ.

  • For everything the app does inside your Jira site, you are the controller and we are your processor. We act on your instructions. The Data Processing Addendum governs it.
  • For the licence and support records we receive about your purchase, we are the controller in our own right. That is covered further down, and it is the only personal data that genuinely reaches us in Côte d'Ivoire.

What the app reads from Jira

DataWhy
Worklogs: author account ID, start date, duration To calculate how much time each person logged on each day. This is the whole purpose of the app.
Users: account ID, display name, avatar URL, active status, account type To list the people a report covers, and to exclude bot and integration accounts, which never owe a timesheet.

The app requests read:jira-work, read:jira-user and storage:app. It holds no permission to create, modify or delete anything in Jira.

One honest qualification. read:jira-work is broader than what the app uses; Atlassian offers no worklog-only alternative. And Jira's worklog objects can carry a free-text comment written by the employee, which may say anything at all. The app discards every field except the author's account ID, the start date and the duration, at the moment the response arrives. Nothing else is used, stored, displayed, exported or logged. We would rather tell you that than claim the app cannot see it.

Who can open the report

Only Jira administrators. The report shows one person's working time to another, so it is not available to ordinary users of your site. Settings and the manual scan are likewise restricted.

What the app stores, and where

Storage uses Atlassian Forge's hosted key-value store, inside the Atlassian infrastructure serving your site. We operate no database and hold no copy.

StoredContentsRetention
Settings Expected hours per working day, public holiday dates, and per-person overrides (hours per day, and whether a person is expected to log time at all), keyed by Atlassian account ID. Until changed or until the app is uninstalled.
Roster Account ID and display name of the people the report covers. Needed because the scheduled scan runs with no signed-in user and cannot list Jira users on its own. Replaced each time an administrator opens the app. Deleted on uninstall.
Weekly history Per week: the week's dates, the number of people checked, the time of the check, and for each person short of their expected hours, their account ID, display name and the shortfall. 26 weeks. Records older than that are deleted automatically, both when the scan runs and whenever an administrator opens the app.

No worklog is ever copied into this store. Hours on screen are read from Jira each time a report runs.

People who leave your organisation remain in the weekly history until their records age out, because the history is a record of weeks that already happened. Removing someone from Jira, or excluding them in Settings, stops them appearing in new reports.

How the app evaluates people

We describe this plainly, because it concerns people's working lives.

Once a week the app totals the hours each person logged over the previous completed week and compares that to the hours their employer configured for them, after deducting weekends, configured public holidays and any per-person exemption. If the total is lower, the person is recorded as short by the difference. The app then counts how many of the most recent consecutive weeks each person has been short, and highlights anyone short for three weeks running.

It is a comparison of two numbers. It knows nothing about leave, sickness, part-time arrangements the employer has not configured, or work done outside Jira, and it is only as accurate as the worklogs your colleagues write. The app takes no decision about anyone. It produces a figure for a human to interpret, and your employer must not base a disciplinary measure on it alone. If a figure about you looks wrong, ask your employer to correct the underlying worklog or the configured expectation.

Where we are, and what that means

We are established in Côte d'Ivoire, outside the European Economic Area, in a country for which the European Commission has issued no adequacy decision.

Your data is not stored there and we hold no copy of it. But under Chapter V of the GDPR, a transfer is about who the data becomes available to, not about where the servers sit, and remote access from a third country counts. Appointing us is therefore a transfer. We rely on the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914, Module Two, controller to processor), incorporated in the Data Processing Addendum. A copy is free on request.

We have carried out a transfer impact assessment, published in full. Its central finding is simple: there is nothing at our end to seize. We hold no copy of your data, no credentials to your site, no server and no database, so we could not comply with a demand for it from any authority, in Côte d'Ivoire or anywhere else.

What we can and cannot reach

We state this precisely instead of absolutely, because absolute claims about vendor access are rarely true of anyone.

  • We hold no credentials to your Atlassian site and cannot sign in to it.
  • Forge gives a developer no interface or command to read a customer installation's stored data. We cannot retrieve your settings, roster or history.
  • We do write the code that runs on your data inside Forge. That is the one real access path, and the commitments covering it are in the Addendum.
  • Forge application logs may be visible to us depending on Atlassian's platform behaviour and your own configuration. The app writes only aggregate counts to its logs, never a name, an account ID or an hours figure, and the scheduled scan catches its own errors so that nothing specific to you reaches a stack trace.

Sub-processors

Atlassian Pty Ltd and its affiliates, which provide the Forge runtime the app executes on and the storage it writes to. You already engage Atlassian directly for Jira itself; we name it here so your own records are complete.

There is no other. The app performs no outbound network call to anything but the Atlassian APIs of your own site, which you can verify yourself: its manifest declares no egress permission, and adding one would require a major version that your administrator would have to approve before it could take effect.

One detail for completeness: avatars shown in the report are loaded by your own browser from the URLs Jira supplies, which we neither choose nor control.

Your rights

For data inside the app, your employer controls it: ask them to access, correct or erase it, and they can do so directly. For the licence and support data above, where we are the controller, you may ask us for access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing and portability, and you may object to processing based on our legitimate interest.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority in your country of residence or work.

If a data subject contacts us about data we hold as processor, we will not answer on the substance; we will refer them to their employer and tell the employer without undue delay.

Deleting everything

Uninstalling the app triggers a cleanup that deletes its settings, roster and history. We retain no copy, because we never held one. Licence records we hold as controller follow the retention stated above.
