Data Processing Addendum

Missing Time for Jira. Last updated 18 October 2026.

This addendum forms part of the End User Licence Agreement and applies where we process personal data on your behalf under the GDPR, the UK GDPR or comparable legislation.

1. Roles and instructions

You are the controller. We are the processor.

We process personal data only on your documented instructions, including with regard to transfers to a third country, unless required to do so by law to which we are subject; in that case we will inform you of the requirement before processing, unless the law prohibits it on important grounds of public interest. Your documented instructions are this addendum, the Licence Agreement, the configuration you set in the app, and any further written instruction you give us.

If an instruction appears to us to infringe data protection law, we will tell you immediately, and may suspend it until it is withdrawn, amended or confirmed.

The nature of the processing is automated reading, calculation, comparison and short-term retention of working-time data. The purposes are those you set: identifying who has logged less time than you expect, and keeping a limited history of that comparison.

2. Subject matter and duration

Reporting on time logged in your Jira site, for as long as the app is installed.

3. Categories of data subject

Users of your Jira site, typically your employees and contractors.

No special category data within the meaning of Article 9 GDPR is processed by design. The app does not use issue summaries, descriptions, comments or attachments, and does not use email addresses.

Two technical facts stated precisely. First, Jira's worklog objects may themselves contain a free-text worklog comment and the author's profile fields. Second, read:jira-work is broader than what the app uses, because Atlassian offers no narrower worklog-only scope. We request the narrowest scope available, not the narrowest imaginable.

You remain responsible for assessing whether patterns in shortfall data could, in your organisation, reveal special category information about someone.

5. Confidentiality

We are an individual developer. No other person has access to the app's source, its deployment pipeline or our Atlassian developer account, which is protected by multi-factor authentication. We are bound by a duty of confidentiality in respect of all personal data processed under this addendum, surviving termination. Were we ever to authorise another person to process personal data on your behalf, we would first bind them in writing to confidentiality and update section 6 before they obtained any access.

6. Sub-processors

You give us your general written authorisation to engage sub-processors, on the conditions in this section.

Our sole sub-processor is Atlassian Pty Ltd and its affiliates, which provide the Forge runtime the app executes on and the key-value store it writes to. You already engage Atlassian directly for Jira itself; we name it here so your records are complete.

We engage no other. The app performs no outbound network call to any system other than the Atlassian APIs of your own site, which is verifiable from the absence of any egress permission in its manifest.

If we intend to add or replace a sub-processor we will notify the technical contact on your licence and publish the change here at least thirty days in advance. You may object on reasonable data protection grounds within that period; if we cannot resolve the objection you may uninstall and terminate. Any sub-processor would be bound by written contract to the same obligations as this addendum, and we remain fully liable to you for its performance.

7. International transfers

7.1 Where the data is

No personal data processed by the app is stored, copied, downloaded or hosted in Côte d'Ivoire or on any system we operate. All processing and storage happen inside the Atlassian Forge infrastructure serving your site. We operate no server, no database and no backup. The manifest declares no egress permission, so the app is technically incapable of sending data outside the Atlassian APIs of your own site; adding such a permission would require a new major version, presented to your administrator for explicit re-approval before it could take effect.

7.2 Why it is nonetheless a transfer

Under Chapter V GDPR, a transfer occurs where a controller or processor subject to the GDPR makes personal data available to an entity in a third country. Remote access from a third country is a transfer even where the data never leaves the EEA. Because we are established in Côte d'Ivoire, a country without an adequacy decision, and act as your processor, appointing us is a transfer notwithstanding section 7.1.

We therefore rely on the Standard Contractual Clauses adopted by Commission Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914 of 4 June 2021, Module Two (controller to processor), which are incorporated into this addendum by reference and form an integral part of it. Where the UK GDPR applies, the UK International Data Transfer Addendum to those Clauses applies. Where Swiss law applies, the Clauses apply with the amendments published by the Swiss Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner. A signed copy is free on request to contact@souciance.com.

For the purposes of the Clauses, the annexes are published here and the docking clause (Clause 7) applies.

In case of conflict, the Standard Contractual Clauses prevail over any other provision of this addendum or of the Licence Agreement, including its governing law and jurisdiction clause.

7.3 Supplementary measures

Following Case C-311/18 (Schrems II) we have carried out a transfer impact assessment, published and reviewed at least annually. Its central finding is that there is nothing at our end to seize: we hold no copy of the data and no means of access to it, so we would be unable to comply with a disclosure demand from any public authority. We additionally commit that:

  • No local copy. We will not download, export or replicate personal data out of the Atlassian infrastructure, by any means, including for debugging.
  • No egress. We will not introduce an outbound network permission without first updating this addendum. Such a change is in any event subject to your administrator's prior approval.
  • No personal data in logs. Logging is restricted to aggregate counts and non-personal diagnostics.
  • Public authority requests. We will handle any such request under Clause 15 of the Clauses: notify you promptly where lawful, challenge it where there are grounds, and disclose the minimum if compelled. We report annually on request, including nil returns.

8. Security (Article 32)

Taking account of the state of the art, the costs, and the nature, scope, context and purposes of the processing, we implement and will not degrade the following measures.

  • Architecture. The app runs entirely within Atlassian Forge. We operate no server, no database and no backup, and hold no credentials to your site.
  • Encryption. In transit and at rest, provided by the Forge platform.
  • Minimisation. The narrowest scopes Atlassian offers for the function. No worklog, no avatar and no email address is stored. Records are keyed on account IDs.
  • Access control. The report, its settings and the manual scan require Jira administrator permission. Our developer account, the only route by which the code can change, uses multi-factor authentication.
  • Logging. Aggregate, non-personal diagnostics only. The scheduled scan catches its own errors so that nothing customer-specific reaches a stack trace.
  • Restoration. Provided by the Forge platform. Most stored data can be rebuilt from your own Jira worklogs.
  • Testing. Each release is reviewed to re-verify that the manifest declares no egress permission and no write scope.

9. Assisting you

9.1 Data subject rights

We assist you, by appropriate technical and organisational measures and so far as possible, in responding to requests under Chapter III GDPR. In practice everything the app holds about a person is visible to you in the app and in your own Jira site, so you can satisfy most requests without us. If a request needs action only we can take, write to contact@souciance.com; we acknowledge within two business days and respond substantively within ten business days, or sooner where your own deadline requires. If a data subject contacts us directly we will not answer on the substance; we refer them to you and tell you without undue delay.

9.2 Articles 32 to 36

We assist you in complying with Articles 32 to 36 GDPR, taking account of the nature of the processing and the information available to us. In particular:

  • Article 35. Because this app involves systematic monitoring of employees and produces a behavioural measure, you are likely to be required to carry out a data protection impact assessment. We publish a DPIA support pack describing the data flows, storage, retention, the scheduled scan and the consecutive weeks calculation, and we answer reasonable written questions about it.
  • Article 36. If you must consult your supervisory authority before processing, we provide the information it asks of you about the app.

10. Personal data breach

We notify you of any personal data breach affecting personal data processed on your behalf without undue delay and in any event within twenty-four hours of becoming aware. We notify the technical contact on your licence and any security contact you give us. We will not notify a supervisory authority or a data subject on your behalf unless you instruct us to.

So far as available to us, the notification describes the nature of the breach including the categories and approximate number of data subjects and records concerned, a contact point, the likely consequences, and the measures taken or proposed. Where we cannot provide all of it at once we provide it in phases rather than delay the first notification.

Where a breach would realistically come from. We hold no copy of your data and no credentials, so we are unlikely to detect one first. Two scenarios are real. An incident at Atlassian affecting Forge, which Atlassian would normally notify you of directly and which we pass on regardless. Or compromise of our Atlassian developer account, the only route by which the code running on your data could be altered; if we have any reason to believe it has been compromised we notify you, inform Atlassian immediately and request suspension of the app, whether or not we have evidence that data was affected.

We document every such breach and make the documentation available to you and your supervisory authority on request.

11. Return and deletion

At your choice, we delete or return all personal data processed on your behalf at the end of the service, and delete existing copies, unless law requires retention.

Deletion is the default: uninstalling triggers a cleanup in which the app deletes its settings, roster and history. We retain no copy at any time because we never hold one.

To receive data back first, export the current report and the weekly history from within the app at any time, free, in a structured and machine-readable form.

12. Audit

We make available all information necessary to demonstrate compliance with Article 28 and this addendum, and we allow for and contribute to audits, including inspections, by you or an auditor you mandate.

In practice, since we operate no systems of our own holding your data, the following normally satisfies an audit and is free on written request: this addendum and its annexes; the manifest showing the scopes requested and the absence of egress; a description of every storage key, its contents and retention; our transfer impact assessment; our DPIA support pack; and our Article 30(2) record as it relates to you.

If that is insufficient, you may request an audit once in any twelve-month period, on thirty days' notice, subject to confidentiality, at your cost, conducted remotely. A supervisory authority exercising its powers is not subject to those limits. Where an audit finds a non-conformity we remediate it at our cost without undue delay.

13. Your responsibilities as controller

This app processes the working time of identified individuals. You are responsible for:

  • determining and documenting a lawful basis. Note that consent is generally not valid in an employment relationship, because of the imbalance of power;
  • carrying out a data protection impact assessment where required, which we expect will usually be the case. We assist under section 9.2;
  • informing the individuals concerned under Articles 13 and 14, including about the weekly history and the consecutive-weeks measure;
  • completing any information, consultation or co-determination procedure with employee representatives required where your people work. This is not a formality: deploying a time-monitoring tool engages the works council in several countries, and in some of them data gathered without that step cannot lawfully be used at all;
  • ensuring no decision producing legal effects or similarly significantly affecting someone, including any disciplinary measure, is taken solely on the app's output;
  • keeping the roster current by opening the app after a departure.
