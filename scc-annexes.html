Annex I.B — Description of the transfer

Categories of data subjects Users of the data exporter's Jira site, typically its employees and contractors.
Categories of personal data Atlassian account ID; display name; avatar URL (read, not stored); time logged per person per day (read, not stored); shortfall against expected hours per week (derived, stored); number of consecutive weeks short (derived, stored); expected hours and exclusion flags set by the exporter (stored).
Sensitive data None is requested or intentionally processed. The app does not use issue content, comments, attachments or email addresses. Jira worklog objects may themselves contain a free-text comment written by the employee; it is discarded at the boundary and never stored, displayed, exported or logged.
Frequency of the transfer Continuous for as long as the app is installed. Reads occur when an administrator opens the report, and once daily when the scheduled scan runs.
Nature of the processing Automated reading, aggregation, comparison against a configured expectation, and short-term retention of the comparison. No decision about any individual is taken by the app.
Purpose Identifying users of the exporter's Jira site who have logged less working time than the exporter expects of them, and retaining a limited history of that comparison so that repeated shortfalls can be distinguished from isolated ones.
Retention Settings and roster: until changed, or until the app is uninstalled. Weekly history: 26 weeks, pruned by age both when the scan runs and when an administrator opens the app. All three are deleted by the app itself on uninstall.
Transfers to sub-processors Atlassian Pty Ltd and its affiliates, which provide the runtime and the storage. For the duration of the installation, for the purpose stated above.

Annex I.C — Competent supervisory authority

The supervisory authority of the Member State in which the data exporter is established. Where the exporter is not established in the EEA but has designated a representative under Article 27 GDPR, the authority of the Member State where that representative is established. Where neither applies, the authority of the Member State in which the data subjects whose personal data is transferred are located.

Annex II — Technical and organisational measures

Measures the importer applies under Article 32 GDPR and Clause 8.6 of the Clauses. Several are properties of the architecture rather than policies, which is noted where it is the case: a property cannot be quietly relaxed, a policy can.

MeasureImplementation
Pseudonymisation and minimisation Stored records are keyed on Atlassian account IDs. No worklog is copied into storage. No avatar and no email address is stored. Worklog objects are reduced to three fields at the moment the API response arrives, so unused fields do not persist even in memory.
Confidentiality Encryption in transit and at rest is provided by the Forge platform for all stored and transmitted data. The importer operates no server, no database and no backup, and holds no credential to the exporter's site.
Access control The report, its settings and the manual scan require Jira administrator permission, enforced server-side in every resolver rather than hidden in the interface. The importer's Atlassian developer account, the only route by which the application code can be changed, is protected by multi-factor authentication.
Limitation of outbound data flow Architectural. The application manifest declares no egress permission, so the Forge runtime blocks any network call other than to the Atlassian APIs of the exporter's own site. Adding such a permission would constitute a major version and would be presented to the exporter's administrator for explicit approval before taking effect.
Limitation of write access Architectural. The manifest requests only read scopes on Jira content. The application holds no permission to create, modify or delete anything in Jira.
Logging Application logs contain aggregate counts and non-personal diagnostics only. The scheduled scan catches its own errors so that no identifier specific to the exporter reaches a stack trace.
Retention and erasure Weekly history is pruned by date, on write and on read, so retention continues to operate even if the scheduled scan stops. An uninstall trigger deletes all three storage keys.
Availability and restoration Provided by the Forge platform. Most stored data is derived and can be rebuilt from the exporter's own Jira worklogs; only the weekly history and the settings cannot.
Testing and review Each release is reviewed to re-verify that the manifest declares no egress permission and no write scope, and that logging remains non-personal.
Measures for sub-processors The sole sub-processor is Atlassian, whose security programme, certifications and data residency options the exporter already engages directly for Jira itself.
